## Abstract

This paper develops the Level-3 cosmological extension of the Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP): the hypothesis that observational evidence normally interpreted as cosmic expansion may involve large-scale **Temporal Shear**. In TEP, matter clocks and photon phases evolve in the causal matter metric \(\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}\), with the conformal clock-rate field \(A(\phi)\) defining the Temporal Shear \(\Sigma_\mu = \nabla_\mu \ln A(\phi)\). Standard cosmology compresses cosmological redshift, distance scaling, and apparent acceleration into the FLRW scale factor \(a(t)\). TEP-COSMO proposes that \(a(t)\) may be an effective variable reconstructed from accumulated Temporal Shear and Temporal Topology along cosmological lines of sight.

The core relation is \(a_{\text{eff}}(\gamma) = \exp[-\int_\gamma \Sigma_\parallel^{\text{eff}} d\ell]\), where \(1+z_T = a_{\text{eff}}^{-1}\). In the homogeneous integrable limit, this reproduces the FLRW relation \(1+z = a_0/a_{\text{em}}\). In the general case, expansion, acceleration, and the inferred Big Bang boundary become features of the reconstruction rather than primitive properties of space.

Utilizing a global Bayesian synthesis of 1,701 Pantheon+ supernovae and Planck 2018 acoustic anchors, the analysis finds that a mixed transport model (\(\epsilon_T = 0.23\)) achieves a modest log-likelihood improvement (\(\Delta \ln \mathcal{L} = 5.3\)) over standard \(\Lambda\)CDM. Standard distance measurements may be biased by violation of the Isochrony Axiom, necessitating a probe-dependent framework where distance and growth observables are fit independently. Structure growth validation using this framework shows consistency with Planck \(\sigma_8\) measurements (1.49\(\sigma\) deviation).

## Introduction: The Geometry of Time

Since 1929, the observation of cosmic redshift has been interpreted as evidence for the physical expansion of space. This interpretation, while mathematically consistent within the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) framework, requires the existence of a singular temporal origin—the Big Bang—and a subsequent evolution dominated by undetected forms of energy. In recent years, the standard model has encountered a significant empirical crisis: the Hubble tension. The $5\sigma$ discrepancy between local and global determinations of $H_0$ suggests that the underlying physical interpretation of redshift may be incomplete.

A more fundamental alternative is proposed: that cosmic expansion is a geometric misinterpretation of accumulated Temporal Shear. The Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP) asserts that the rate of time is a dynamical field governed by the conformal clock-rate factor \(A(\phi)\), and that global synchronization is path-dependent. In such a geometry, redshift is not caused primarily by stretching of space, but by open-path accumulation of Temporal Shear along the emitter-observer light path.

This paper introduces Temporal Shear Cosmology: the hypothesis that the observational evidence normally interpreted as cosmic expansion, acceleration, and a Big Bang origin is instead the large-scale reconstruction of accumulated Temporal Shear. The analysis shows how the low-redshift Hubble law, supernova time dilation, Tolman scaling, distance duality, and acoustic-anchor projection can be formulated without treating spatial expansion as primitive. By replacing the expansion-based scale factor with the Temporal Shear projection \(\Sigma_\parallel^{\text{eff}}\), the Hubble tension is reinterpreted, and the Big Bang is recovered as an effective integrable reconstruction of a stable, non-integrable temporal geometry. Temporal Shear Cosmology refers to the physical framework; TEP-COSMO refers to the associated inference pipeline used to compare primitive expansion models against Temporal Shear reconstruction models.

Standard cosmology assumes the **Isochrony Axiom**: proper time accumulation is independent of local gravitational environment. Under TEP, this axiom is violated. \[ a_{\text{eff}}(\gamma) = \exp \left[ -\int_\gamma \left( \Sigma_\parallel + \mathcal{C}_T \right) d\ell \right] \]

## Discussion: Interpretation and Limitations

The evidence presented in this paper suggests that the Temporal Shear framework may provide an alternative interpretation of cosmological observations that are conventionally attributed to kinematic expansion. However, several important limitations and interpretational issues must be acknowledged.

### Probe-Dependent Framework

A key theoretical insight is that standard cosmological distance measurements (Cepheid variables, Type Ia supernovae) may be systematically biased by violation of the Isochrony Axiom—the assumption that proper time accumulation is independent of local gravitational environment. Under TEP, Cepheids in deep gravitational potentials experience period contraction, which when interpreted through the standard Period-Luminosity relation mimics diminished luminosity and leads to underestimated distances.

This necessitates a probe-dependent \(\epsilon_T\) framework, where distance measurements and structure growth measurements are fit independently. The fitted \(\epsilon_T = 0.23\) from SNe data may compensate for this distance bias rather than representing the true TEP effect on structure growth. Structure growth validation using \(\epsilon_T = 0\) for growth calculations shows consistency with Planck \(\sigma_8\) measurements (1.49\(\sigma\) deviation), suggesting that the TEP effect on growth may be smaller than the effect on distances.

### Statistical Evidence

The mixed transport model (M1) achieves a log-likelihood improvement of \(\Delta \ln \mathcal{L} = 5.3\) over standard \(\Lambda\)CDM. While this is positive, the improvement is modest and does not constitute decisive statistical evidence. The improvement may reflect the additional flexibility of the model rather than a decisive preference for the TEP framework. Further data and more sophisticated statistical tests are needed to establish stronger evidence.
