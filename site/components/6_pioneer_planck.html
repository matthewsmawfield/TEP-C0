6. Empirical Tests: Pioneer and Planck

6.1 Pioneer 10/11 Anomalous Acceleration

The Pioneer 10 and 11 spacecraft exhibited an anomalous sunward acceleration of approximately aP ≈ (8.74 ± 1.33) × 10-10 m/s2 after they passed 20 AU from the Sun. Standard explanations (thermal recoil from RTGs, gas leaks) have been invoked but fail to fully account for the magnitude and constancy of the effect.

In the TEP-COSMO framework, the Pioneer anomaly is not a spacecraft systematic but a direct measurement of the Sun's macroscopic temporal shear gradient. As the spacecraft recedes, it traverses regions of decreasing A(φ), and its proper-time oscillator frequency shifts relative to the Earth-based reference. The observed Doppler residual is the recovery curve of this gradient:

aP(r) = c2 ∇r ln A(φ(r))

While the primary TEP-C0 analysis bounds the global temporal shear amplitude to zero in dense environments (preserving the ΛCDM acoustic peak structure via the conformal boundary), a more radical extension of the theory is currently under development for TEP-C1.

In this future framework, TEP-COSMO will attempt to derive the acoustic structure without invoking a separate cold dark matter component. Instead, the relative heights of the odd and even acoustic peaks would be determined by the ratio of baryonic matter to the total temporal shear energy at recombination. The shear field, being non-dissipative, could theoretically mimic the effect of cold dark matter on the photon-baryon fluid oscillations.

where Ωshear(φ) replaces Ωc. This branch (CMB-replacement TEP) requires an extensive modification to the CLASS Boltzmann solver and is left as future work, keeping the current TEP-C0 results strictly focused on the CMB-boundary limit.

6.3 Joint Local and Cosmological Constraints (Future Work)

Ultimately, the combination of Pioneer (local solar system) and Planck (cosmological) data will provide a cross-scale test of the extended TEP framework. Both phenomena are governed by the same conformal factor A(φ) but evaluated at vastly different density scales. Proving that a single parameter can simultaneously fit both datasets remains the ultimate objective for the TEP-C1 analysis pipeline.
