6.7 Falsifiable Predictions

The following near-term observational tests would strengthen or falsify the TEP-C0 temporal-shear framework in the late-time distance-redshift sector.

  1. Environment-dependent Cepheid period shifts. Within TEP, the scalar-field mass-screening in massive host galaxies should produce a systematic, directionally oriented shift in Cepheid pulsation periods relative to the field. The TEP-C0 host-environment prediction aligns the massive-host-brighter direction with the established astrophysical mass step, but a quantitative amplitude prediction from the screened scalar-field geometry ($\Delta P/P \sim 10^{-4}$–$10^{-3}$ for typical host overdensity contrasts) would be testable with JWST/NIRCam Cepheid samples at $z \sim 0.01$–$0.05$ if environment overdensity is recorded.
  2. Redshift-space distortion signature at $z \sim 0.5$. In the operative screened branch the TEP deviation is small and positive: the TEP-HC active-perturbation run gives $f\sigma_8(z{=}0.5) = 0.4129$ versus $0.4097$ for $\Lambda$CDM ($\Delta f\sigma_8 \approx +0.003$, $\sigma_8 \approx 0.824$), and the C0 joint-background evaluation returns $\Delta f\sigma_8 \approx +0.002$–$+0.011$ for $\epsilon_T \le 0.05$ (Step 06-05). DESI Year-1 RSD precision ($\sigma(f\sigma_8) \sim 0.02$ at $z \sim 0.5$) therefore treats this sector as a consistency gate rather than a near-term discriminator: a measured $f\sigma_8$ exceeding $\Lambda$CDM by more than $\sim 0.02$ at $2$–$3\sigma$ would already begin to disfavour the unscreened diagnostic branch, while the operative branch predicts near-$\Lambda$CDM growth with a mild positive offset.
  3. ISW–galaxy cross-correlation systematic enhancement at intermediate $z$. The TEP conformal scalar field contributes an additional source to the Integrated Sachs–Wolfe effect through the time-varying $A_{\rm dyn}(z)$ potential. This predicts a systematic enhancement in the ISW–galaxy cross-correlation at $z \sim 0.5$ relative to $\Lambda$CDM, driven by the modified late-time potential evolution rather than by a cosmological constant. The effect is expected to be modest (order-unity percent to few-percent level) and testable with DESI LRG samples cross-correlated with Planck CMB lensing maps, though cosmic variance and foreground systematics make this a qualitative directional probe rather than a precision discriminator.
  4. Supernova time-dilation drift. The TEP redshift reconstruction $1+z = A_0/A_{\rm em}$ predicts that supernova light-curve time-dilation factors should track the conformal clock map rather than the standard kinematic $1+z$ at the $<0.1\%$ level for $z \lesssim 2$. A sample of $>10^3$ spectroscopically confirmed SNe Ia with light-curve shape parameters measured to $\sim$1% precision could detect this deviation.
  5. Evolving dark-energy equation-of-state projection $(w_0,w_a)$ in DESI BAO + SNe combinations. Two distinct effective equation-of-state channels arise depending on whether the screened homogeneous background or the intermediate-scale supernova transport kernel is projected onto a standard FLRW dark-energy fluid: (i) on the screened homogeneous hi_class background ($\epsilon_T^{\rm HC}=0.0043\pm0.0025$, $z_T=5.0$, $n_T=2.0$; Paper 18), $H_{\rm TEP}(z)=M(z)H_{\Lambda{\rm CDM}}(z)$ remains within $0.4\%$ of $\Lambda$CDM at $z<0.5$ and yields a near-cosmological-constant trajectory $w_{\rm eff}(z)\simeq-1.000$ at $z<0.5$ ($-0.987$ at $z=1$, CPL fit $w_0\simeq-1.04\pm0.02$, $w_a\simeq+0.15\pm0.08$ over $z\in[0.01,2]$); whereas (ii) when the zero-$\Lambda$ M1 line-of-sight temporal-shear transport kernel $d\chi/dz=(c/H_0)[1+\epsilon_{\rm shear}^{\rm los}\ln(1+z)\exp(-(z/z_T)^2)]/(1+z)^{3/2}$ ($\epsilon_{\rm shear}^{\rm los}=0.8708$, $z_T=5.0$) is analyzed under a standard FLRW dark-energy parameterization $E_{\rm eff}^2(z)=(1+z)^3/\gamma(z)^2=\Omega_{m,0}(1+z)^3+(1-\Omega_{m,0})f_{\rm DE}(z)$ alongside the acoustic matter fraction $\Omega_{m,0}=\omega_{m,\rm eff}/h^2=0.3038$ derived in Paper 18 (or Planck $\Omega_{m,0}=0.315$), its effective equation of state $w_{\rm eff}(z)=-1+\tfrac{1+z}{3}d\ln f_{\rm DE}/dz$ yields $w_{\rm eff}(0)=-0.834$ to $-0.848$ and a linear CPL fit across the DESI BAO lever arm $z\in[0,1]$ of $(w_0=-0.71,\,w_a=-0.87)$ at $\Omega_{m,0}=0.304$ and $(w_0=-0.69,\,w_a=-1.13)$ at $\Omega_{m,0}=0.315$ (Paper 27, Step 03). This reproduces the exact $(w_0>-1,\,w_a<0)$ thawing dark-energy evolution reported by DESI Year-1 BAO + CMB + DESY5 SNe ($w_0=-0.727\pm0.067$, $w_a=-1.05^{+0.31}_{-0.27}$) to within $0.28\sigma$–$0.61\sigma$ without adding free CPL parameters or invoking phantom energy. On the single-history native background that fits Pantheon+, DESI DR2 BAO and the Planck acoustic angle together (Step 03-14, $\Delta\chi^2=-8.1$ against $\Lambda$CDM), the same thawing direction appears as $(w_0,w_a)\approx(-0.83,-0.44)$, with $w_{\rm eff}\approx-0.86$ near $z\approx0.4$ returning to $-1$ by $z\approx1.5$.

Each prediction is quantitative and tied to a specific observable. A null result in tests 2 or 3 at $>3\sigma$ would constrain the TEP late-time amplitude to the screened limit ($\epsilon_T \to 0$), while a positive detection would provide independent evidence for temporal-shear transport.
