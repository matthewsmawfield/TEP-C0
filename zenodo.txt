This paper presents a direct empirical challenge to the necessity of primitive cosmic expansion. In the Temporal Equivalence Principle framework, observed redshift is reconstructed as conformal proper-time transport, 1+z=A₀/Aₑₘ rather than as stretching of a spatial scale factor. Standard cosmology interprets observational redshift and luminosity distance scaling as evidence of a stretching spatial metric, parameterized by the Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) scale factor a(t). The observational role played by the FLRW scale factor is mapped, within the TEP conformal-frame construction, onto the temporal clock-rate field A(φ). In the tested late-time background sector, the perceived acceleration normally attributed to dark energy, Λ, is reconstructed as the kinematic clock-drift rate contribution of the Temporal Shear field, Ω_φ, in the static-manifold distance-redshift Jacobian (rather than a w = +1 Friedmann fluid driving metric expansion a(t)).

The core relation is 1+z = A₀/Aₑₘ. In the static conformal interpretation developed here, intergalactic separations are not treated as primitively expanding; the apparent expansion is reconstructed through temporal transport. In this framework, the limit conventionally written as a→0 is re-expressed as A_clock→0: a TEP temporal-horizon boundary of observational clock transport, not a zero-volume spatial singularity. The temporal-horizon background and linear-mode closure are supplied by companion papers TEP-TH and TEP-HC; C0 imports and cross-checks the TEP-HC linear-growth output while focusing on the empirical supernova-sector test.

Using 1,701 Pantheon+ Type Ia supernova measurements (1,543 distinct events) with the full covariance matrix and the official peculiar-velocity-corrected Hubble-diagram redshifts, a pure conformal reconstruction exactly reproduces the ΛCDM homogeneous distance-modulus relation, demonstrating that the background Hubble diagram does not uniquely select an expanding spatial metric. More strongly, the conservative physical no-Λ temporal-shear branch with fixed line-of-sight turnover zₗₒₛ=5 (using the acoustic-sector scale as a conservative reference) improves the standardized supernova likelihood by Δχ² ≃ -3.81 relative to baseline ΛCDM and achieves a Bayes factor of approximately 5.8, nominally in the "substantial" band of the Jeffreys scale — reported alongside its prior-sensitivity grid, and its reduction to BF =0.52–0.96 after low-redshift removal (z>0.05–0.01): the improvement localizes to the 77 SH0ES calibrators and the z>1 tail, so the SNe-only preference is evidence for a transport-shaped deviation whose physical attribution is tested by the joint and environmental channels. Under the canonical suppression exponent n_T=2 the free-zₗₒₛ branch marginalizes the turnover scale, with its likelihood maximum at the admissible turnover zₗₒₛ≃5.3 and its posterior volume extending over the nearly flat unsuppressed tail, and returns BF ≈ 5.4, while the fixed zₗₒₛ=100 benchmark gives a comparable BF ≈ 5.4 (Δχ² ≃ -3.69); the admissible branches thus saturate at a moderate, internally consistent level rather than at extreme amplification, and both standard dark-energy extensions (wCDM, CPL) return weaker evidence at matched or greater freedom. The conservative physical model with zₗₒₛ=5—which turns over before the acoustic epoch and is therefore consistent with the joint CMB bound on the homogeneous shear component—demonstrates that a matter-only temporal-shear geometry is competitive with ΛCDM in the late-time SNe distance-redshift sector.
